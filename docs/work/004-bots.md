## Plan

Agreed with the owner on 2026-11-03 and reviewed by a second agent over three rounds (approved): bots fill empty seats (so two friends can play a fuller table) and take over for players who disconnect. They should play like an average player — sensible, not hard to beat. Same working rules as [`feedback-plan.md`](feedback-plan.md) ("Working rules": one commit per step with `npm test` passing, `progress.md` updated after each step, usage checked, park above 85%). Progress is tracked in `progress.md` under "Round 4". The owner asked for the plan to be reviewed by another agent (up to 3 rounds), then implemented without waiting for approval.

### Decisions

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bot seats | The host adds bots in the lobby (`addBot`) and can remove them (`removeBot {player}`). A bot is a normal player with `bot:true`, always ready, no session token, never host. Names are normal first names from a fixed pool with a suffix, e.g. "Ana (Bot)"; a pool name whose first name matches someone at the table is skipped. Humans can't pick a name ending in "(Bot)". |
| 2 | Takeover | A bot plays for an **offline** human (no live stream connection): automatically once they have been offline for 60 s while the game waits on them (their turn, a discard or the starting roll — not an unanswered offer) and at least one other human is connected, or at once when any connected player presses "Let a bot play" on their seat (`takeover {player}`). The seat is marked `auto:true` and shows as "<name> (Bot)" (the owner's example "Marko (Bot)"). The player gets the seat back the moment they reconnect or send any command (`release`, applied in the same transaction). Logged as history events `takeover` / `release`. A taken-over host can't start a rematch; the finished game waits for them. |
| 3 | Rules doc | `CLAUDE.md` and `RULES.md` replace "never invent moves / no bots" with: moves are only made for bot seats, and for a human seat while a bot plays it (handed over by someone at the table, or automatically after 60 s offline while the game waits on that player and at least one other human is connected); no forfeits; a returning player gets their seat back at once. |
| 4 | No cheating | The bot decides from `view(g,p)` only — the same snapshot a human client gets (no opponents' hands, hidden VP or deck order). Engine helpers (`legalSettlements`, `legalRoads`, `tradeRatio`, `routePath`, `score`) work on a view because they only read `board`/`awards`. |
| 5 | Strength | Average: plans one build ahead, no card counting, a little randomness in choices (setup picks among the top few spots, accepts most but not all good trades). Measured against the simple scripted player in `matches.test.mjs` and checked by playing it in the browser. |
| 6 | Pace | Bots act after a short pause so people can follow (about 0.7–1.5 s per move, longer for setup and rolls). `BOT_PACE` env (multiplier, default 1) lets tests run them fast. |
| 7 | Trades | Bots answer human offers (accept/decline), may post one simple 1-for-1 offer per turn when it would complete a build, wait for answers (up to ~20 s, or until everyone answered), then confirm with whoever accepted or cancel. They answer counters made to them when active. They don't counter. No trades in a P2 phase (engine rule). |

### Design

#### Engine (`lib/engine.mjs`)
- `addBot` (host, lobby, seat free): `addPlayer(g,'bot-'+…,name)` + `bot:true`, `ready:true`. Bot ids are generated by the engine from the injected `random` so it stays deterministic.
- `removeBot {player}` (host, lobby, target is a bot): remove from `players`; reassign `color=COLORS[i]` by index so seats stay compact.
- `settings` un-readies only non-host **humans**; bots stay ready. `rematch` re-adds bots as bots (`addPlayer(…,bot)`, ready) and drops `auto`.
- `takeover {player}`: phase not `lobby`/`finished`, target is a human other than the caller, not already `auto`. Sets `auto:true`, logs `takeover {p:target,by:caller}` (`by` is null for the automatic one: the server applies it as the target's seat with `auto:true`, which HTTP commands may not carry). The "is offline" check lives in the server, inside the same transaction (the engine doesn't know connections). Handled before the "Wait for your turn" check.
- `release`: by the seat itself when `auto`; clears it, logs `release {p}`. Allowed in any phase (before the finished check). Winning clears every `auto` so the end screen shows real names. Seat indexes in `takeover`/`removeBot` must be integers.
- `pending(g)` (exported): players who owe input now — `order`: candidates not done; `discard`: players with `discards[i]>0`; `action` with `role===1`: the active player, plus each other player who can still answer an open offer addressed to them (not in `accepted`/`declined`); every other in-game phase: the active player. Used by the bot runner.
- `view()` exposes `bot` and `auto` per player.
- `addPlayer` rejects human names ending in "(bot)" (a flag lets `addBot` through).

#### Bot brain (new `lib/bot.mjs`)
`decide(v, random, ctx)` → one command `{type,...}`, or `{wait:ms}` when the bot is waiting on answers to its own offer. `v` is `view(g,p)`; `ctx.offerAge` is ms since the bot's open offer was posted (the server tracks it). Pure, no I/O.

- **Spot value** `spotValue(v,vertex,p)`: sum of pips of adjacent producing hexes (robber hex counts less), bonus for resources the bot doesn't produce yet, small bonus for a port matching a strong resource; small random jitter.
- **Setup**: settlement among the top 3 legal spots (weighted toward the best); road on the edge that leads toward the best spot two steps away.
- **Target**: the next build, chosen each call: city (if a settlement and cities < 4) > settlement (if a legal spot) > road toward the best open spot (if no legal settlement spot) > development card. "Missing" = cards short for the target.
- **preRoll**: play a Knight if the robber is on one of its producing hexes or it would take Largest Army; else roll.
- **Action**, one command per call, in order: play a useful dev card (Road Building if roads are possible, Invention for missing cards, Monopoly on the resource it needs most, Knight as above); build the target if affordable; a supply/port trade if it completes the target this turn (give surplus, best ratio); one player offer per turn (1 surplus for 1 missing, to everyone); confirm/cancel/wait on its offer; buy a development card with spare cards when the hand is over the discard limit; else end.
- **Robber**: hex not touching its own buildings, maximising pips × opposing buildings, weighted toward the leader (public score); never the desert. **Steal**: the victim with the highest public score (then most cards).
- **Discard**: keep the cards for the target; discard from the most-held types first.
- **Monopoly / Invention / free roads**: the resource it needs most / missing cards for the target / roads toward the best spot.
- **Answering offers**: accept when it gets a missing card and gives a non-missing one, does not lose cards it needs, and the offerer's public score is below `goal-2`; otherwise decline. About one good trade in eight is declined anyway (average player). As active player, accept a counter by the same test.
- **Per-turn limits come from the view**, so `decide` stays pure: it counts its own events this turn (`v.events`, `turn===v.turn`, `p===me`): at most one player offer, three supply trades and 30 moves per turn, then it ends the turn.
- **Rules it respects**: one dev card per turn, never one bought this turn; Invention takes exactly `min(2, supply)`; no offers or answers in role 2; supply trades never give away a card the target needs (no ping-pong).
- **Safety**: if `decide` or `apply` throws, the runner uses `fallback(v)` (first legal option for the phase, `end`, decline) and logs the error. If that fails too, the runner logs it and leaves the room alone until the next commit (no tight loop).

#### Server (`server.mjs`)
- After every commit (command, join, takeover) and at startup for every unfinished room: `wake(roomId)`.
- `wake`: one timer per room (`unref()`). Reads the room; if a bot/auto seat is in `pending(g)`, waits the pause for that kind of move, then inside `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`: re-read, recompute `pending`, ask **every** pending bot seat in turn and apply the first real command (so other bots can answer an offer the active bot is waiting on), with `offerId` from `randomBytes` for offers; `revision++`, save; then broadcast and `wake` again. If every seat returns `{wait:ms}`, re-check after the shortest wait. Stale decisions can't happen because each move is decided from the state read inside the transaction.
- Presence: `offlineSince` per room and player (in memory; at startup everyone counts from startup). A 5 s interval checks rooms seen since startup: a human the game waits on (the active player outside unanswered offers, a discard, or the starting roll — not merely an unanswered offer) who has been offline 60 s, while at least one other human is connected, gets `takeover` (`auto:true`, by null). HTTP commands never carry `auto` (the server strips it). At startup, rooms with bot or auto seats are woken.
- Offer ages: `Map(offerId → first-seen time)`, pruned when offers disappear.
- `takeover` arrives as a normal command from a human seat; the server rejects it (400) if the target has a live SSE connection.
- On SSE connect, and before any signed-in command from an `auto` seat, the server applies `release` inside a transaction (revision++, save), then broadcasts and wakes. So a human and a bot never move the same seat.
- `BOT_PACE` env scales the pauses (tests use a small value).

#### Client (`public/app.js`)
- Lobby: host sees "Add a bot" (`data-command="addBot"`) while seats are free, and a remove button on each bot seat (`data-remove-bot="<i>"`); bot seats carry `data-bot` and say "Bot" instead of "Seat n".
- Game: bot and auto seats show "(Bot)" after the name and a small bot badge in the sidebar; for an offline human (from the stream's `online` list) a "Let a bot play" button (`data-takeover="<i>"`). History lines for `takeover`/`release`.
- `retryContext()`: bots move often, so add (a) any command by the active player during `action` (including `confirmOffer`/`cancelOffer`) while `turn/active/role/phase` are unchanged (others can only answer offers then, which never invalidates the active player's own move; the engine re-checks the rest), and (b) `discard` while the player still owes the same number of cards.

#### Tests
- `engine.test.mjs`: add/remove bots (host only, lobby only, seat limit, names, colours re-packed), settings keep bots ready, rematch keeps bots, takeover/release rules, `pending()` per phase, human names ending in "(Bot)" rejected.
- `test/sim.mjs` (shared helper): seeded random, the scripted player (declines every offer), and `play()` which loops over `pending()` with a simulated clock (one second per command) feeding `ctx.offerAge`, bumping `revision` per command and capping the number of commands.
- New `bot.test.mjs`: (0) leak test: shuffling opponents' hands and the deck does not change the bot's command for the same seed; (1) complete games with 2–6 bots for several seeds, decided from views only, with JSON round-trips and supply conservation — every command legal (`apply` never throws); (2) strength: bots vs the scripted player from `matches.test.mjs` (moved to a shared test helper) over a batch of seeded games — loose bounds only: every game finishes, game length plausible, bots win roughly 55–90% of seats-weighted games (it shows they play sensibly, not that they're too strong; tuning lives in `scripts/bot-stats.mjs` and the browser play-test); (3) offers: a human-style offer that helps a bot is accepted, a bad one declined.
- `server.test.mjs`: host + 2 bots with a small `BOT_PACE`: bots roll for order and place in setup on their own; takeover of an offline guest (rejected while that guest is connected) makes a bot play for them; reconnecting releases the seat.
- `browser.test.mjs`: host adds a bot in the lobby (seat shows "(Bot)"), removes and re-adds, starts; the bot rolls and places without help; the human's next turn arrives.
- `scripts/bot-stats.mjs` (not a test): runs many bot games and prints finish rate, turn counts, win share by seat and per strategy — for the strength check and future tuning.
